# Pastoral & Change-Management Critique: *Disciple Making Lab*

## 1. Executive Verdict

As a practitioner who has led staff teams, elder boards, and multi-generational congregations through multi-year culture shifts, my verdict on the **Disciple Making Lab** (C&MA Central District) is straightforward: **the theology and pedagogy are strong, but the organizational change sequencing is dangerously inverted.**

The curriculum rightly refuses to reduce discipleship to curriculum consumption or Sunday attendance. Its core architecture—pairing broad cultural alignment with peer-to-peer apprenticeship, simple visual tools, and iterative Beta testing—solves genuine local church stagnation. 

However, as a 10-month church-transformation engine, the Lab suffers from four structural blind spots:
1. **Unrealistic production bandwidth** between Modules 5 and 6 that will overwhelm bivocational pastors, solo pastors, and lay teams.
2. **Upside-down change sequencing** that delays stakeholder mapping, communication planning, and a current-culture audit until **Module 8**—creating "secret lab" resentment among elders and staff.
3. **Naive ministry integration** that treats legacy small groups, Sunday liturgy, and Next-Gen ministries as downstream recipients rather than co-architects, while omitting doctrinal and character guardrails.
4. **An abrupt Month 13 Cliff**, commissioning exhausted teams as "Culture Builders" right before staff turnover, annual budget friction, and congregational fatigue dismantle early gains.

## 2. What Solves Real Church Pain

Before dismantling its blind spots, credit belongs where the Lab genuinely diagnoses and treats real church dysfunction:

* **Bridging the Culture vs. Mentoring Divide (The Bowtie Framework, pp. 4, 19, 74):** Most churches trap themselves in either Sunday-heavy programming (`Attend 100% -> Connect 60% -> Serve 30%`) or hyper-individualized mentoring disconnected from congregation-wide life. Placing **Culture Making (`PLAN`: Practices, Language, Artifacts, Narratives)** on the left wing and **Relational Apprenticeship (`PATH`: Destination, Map, Vehicle, Tools)** on the right wing—aiming for the `2%` making disciples to ignite a `16%` organizational tipping point—gives boards and staff a shared diagnostic map.
* **Theological Destination Before Tactics (Module 1, pp. 7–10):** Anchoring the definition of a disciple in Romans 8:28–30, Matthew 22:37, 2 Corinthians 3:18, and 2 Corinthians 5:14–20 (*"One called by, in love with, becoming like, and on mission with Christ"*) and separating **Being with Jesus** (Character) from **Doing the things Jesus could do** (Competency) forces congregations to confront the gap between religious activity and Christlikeness.
* **101-Level, "Napkin" Transferability (Modules 1, 3–5):** Most laypeople never disciple anyone because churches hand them intimidating, multi-volume workbooks. The Lab's progression (`Practice -> Tool -> Outcome`), `Picture / Mirror / Window` design lens (p. 43), and simple visual tools—the **Bible Reading Tool Circle** (`Chew / Digest / Act`), **Prayer Tool Wheel** (six Lord's Prayer movements anchored in `Worship-based, Scripture-fed, Spirit-led`), and **Jesus Pattern Mountain** (`UP / IN / OUT`)—make everyday spiritual reproduction believable for ordinary believers.
* **The MAP (`Apprenticeship Square`, p. 13) & `Training Triangle` (p. 23):** Pairing `Information / Imitation / Innovation` with the four developmental stages (`D1/L1` *"I do, you watch"* through `D4/L4` *"You do, I cheer"*) and naming exact stalling temptations—`'I quit' (Valley of the shadow)` at D2, `Co-dependence` at D3, `'The journey is over'` at D4—gives pastors actionable coaching language.
* **Honest Diagnostics & Leader Soul Care:** Separating internal vs. external agency in the **Spiritual SWOT** (p. 5), warning against high-competency/low-character leaders in the **Character & Competency Matrix** (p. 6), requiring a 3-month **Beta** before full rollout (p. 71), and embedding **Soul Care** alongside Onboarding and Ongoing Training (pp. 77–78) reflect genuine pastoral wisdom.

## 3. Organizational Friction & Failure Modes ("Tearing It Apart")

Where the Lab leaves the whiteboard and hits the living organism of a local church, serious structural friction emerges:

### Existing Small Groups: The Unaddressed Two-Tier Collision
Module 6 instructs teams to invent a brand-new **Vehicle** (*"WHAT IS IT CALLED? NAME THE BABY!"*, p. 68) with nine custom structural parameters, a three-part meeting flow (`Celebrate` 10 min / `Discipleship Focus` / `Next Step` 10 min, p. 69), and a four-week repeatable rhythm (p. 70). Meanwhile, existing `"Groups"` appear only as one column inside the `Culture-Making Grid` (pp. 75–76, 84, 90, 108). 

There is **zero structured pathway** for how existing home groups, sermon-based life groups, or legacy Sunday School classes convert into, run alongside, or sunset in favor of this new Vehicle. In practice, launching 3–6 shiny Beta groups whose leaders *"wear lanyards on Sundays"* (Module 9 example, p. 101) manufactures an instant two-tier caste system: elite "disciple-makers" versus second-class social small groups. Veteran small-group leaders will feel devalued and dig in their heels.

### Sunday Services: Programmatic Bloat vs. Liturgy
In the Module 9 Impact Roadmap example (p. 101), `"Worship Service"` integration boils down to monthly testimony slots, quarterly `"Tool Time"` teaching segments, and mentioning Sunday lanyards. Treating Sunday morning primarily as a promotional billboard for the Lab's internal artifacts creates tonal whiplash for guests (`Attend 100%`) and strains preaching calendars. Without integrating the Lab's practices into sermon series structure, communion, baptism narratives, and liturgy, Sunday `"Tool Time"` quickly feels like one more programmatic announcement crowding the morning.

### Children's & Student Ministries: Downstream Retrofitting (Module 7, p. 83)
Module 7's `Tool Integration Practice` asks teams to drop `Discipleship Tool #1` and `#2` into Children's and Student Ministries—ignoring Tools #3–#6—with three broad reflection questions. Next-Gen pastors already survive on razor-thin volunteer margins, child-safety compliance, parent communication, and purchased scope-and-sequence curricula. Asking them in Month 7 to retrofit adult-designed napkin diagrams for preschoolers, middle schoolers, and high schoolers—especially if they weren't at the design table during Modules 1–6—invites polite compliance in the meeting and quiet abandonment on Monday morning.

### Leader Development, Metrics & Missing Guardrails
Module 6 crams `Onboarding Training`, `Ongoing Training (Repeatable Year)`, and `Soul Care` into two dense pages (pp. 77–78) right alongside Toolkit presentations, Vehicle design, Beta logistics, and Culture-Making Grids. Worse, critical operational guardrails are absent:
* **No Character or Doctrinal Gatekeeping:** Module 1's Matrix warns that low-character, high-competency leaders bring *"unlimited potential for kingdom harm"* (p. 6). Yet as Betas multiply toward 2nd- and 3rd-generation peer-led groups using simplified 101 tools, the workbook offers zero leader vetting criteria, theological orthodoxy checks, conflict-escalation protocols, or off-boarding mechanisms.
* **No Soul Care Capacity Math:** Asking *"How will leaders know they have relational access to the life of the leaders investing in them?"* (p. 78) works for 4 Beta leaders. At the `20%` congregational milestone, who actually shepherds 15–30 group leaders without collapsing the Senior Pastor?
* **Vanity Targets & Unfinished Math:** Beta evaluation simply asks for *"3-5 specific ways you'll know"* it is working (p. 71). Module 9 anchors success in `1 functioning 3rd-generation discipleship group`, `20% of the church membership` participating, and `20%` using tools weekly (p. 101)—while leaving the **`1-Year Objective` completely blank (`""`) even in its own worked example!** There are zero leading indicators tracking D2/L2 `"I quit"` attrition, leader burnout, evangelistic fruit (`OUT`), or volunteer cannibalization across existing ministries.

## 4. Change-Management, Sequencing & Bandwidth Critique

### The Fatal Module 8 Inversion ("Secret Lab" Syndrome)
The curriculum's gravest change-management flaw sits in **Module 8**. Only *after* seven months of defining a disciple (M1–2), auditing `PATH` (M5), inventing 5–6 custom visual tools (M4–5), building a new Vehicle (M6), establishing leader care (M6), launching live Betas (M6–7), and planning Next-Gen integration (M7) does the workbook finally introduce:
* **`PLAN INVENTORY: "WHAT IS"`** (p. 89)
* **`COMMUNICATION FOLIO — CONTENT: ONE-PAGER`** (p. 92)
* **`COMMUNICATION FOLIO — PEOPLE: STAKEHOLDERS & TIMELINE`** (p. 93)

In a real church, waiting until Month 8 to audit `"What Is"` and map `"Stakeholders & Timeline"` guarantees institutional pushback:
* **Elder Board Ambush:** Redefining what constitutes a mature disciple and overhauling leadership pipelines are *governance* issues. When an elder board receives a finished One-Pager and Stakeholder Timeline in Month 8—after Betas have already been running since Module 6—it feels like a *fait accompli*. Elders become defensive, nitpick definitions, or halt rollout to reassert spiritual oversight.
* **Staff Turf & "Secret Lab" Alienation:** Non-lab staff watch an inner circle disappear to two-day retreats, test cryptic tools on selected members, and adopt insider vocabulary (`Bowtie`, `MAP`, `PATH/PLAN`). By Module 8, siloed staff experience integration grids not as collaborative vision, but as an uninvited takeover of their ministries.
* **Additive Clutter Instead of Subtraction:** Auditing `"What Is"` in Module 8 means teams spend Modules 4–7 piling new tools and a brand-new Vehicle onto an already maxed-out church calendar. Even though the `Moonshot` diagram includes a `STOP` marker (p. 14), the curriculum contains **zero structured exercises** requiring churches to eliminate legacy programs, meetings, or budget lines to fund the new culture.

### Sustainability & Bandwidth Reality Test
For bivocational pastors, solo pastors, and lay teams working secular 45-hour weeks, the workload distribution is unsustainable:
* **Two 2-Day Immersions + Monthly Sessions:** Module 1 runs Day 1 (`1:00–9:00 PM`) and Day 2 (`7:30 AM–3:30 PM`); Module 6 specifically schedules **Thursday (`1:00–9:00 PM`) and Friday (`8:30 AM–3:30 PM`)**, forcing lay leaders and bivocational pastors to burn scarce PTO before counting travel.
* **The Module 5–6 Production Bottleneck:** After interviewing 6 people (M1), soliciting feedback (M2), and field-testing individual tools (M3–4), Module 5 pushes teams through Tools #2–#5 in one session and then orders them to **"DESIGN/REFINE YOUR REMAINING TOOLS (5 OR 6 TOTAL)"** within a single month for Module 6 presentations (p. 61). Immediately afterward, Module 6 expects them to send Beta invites, **"FORMAT VISUAL TOOLS INTO SHAREABLE PLATFORM"** (p. 80), and schedule Beta leader training—requiring graphic design, digital publishing, and recruiting bandwidth small churches simply lack.
* **Oral-Dependent Documentation:** Across Modules 1–5 and 9, **30 of the 113 workbook pages** are blank or notes-only pages (including 6 in M2, 7 in M5, and 6 in M9). If a key lay team member misses a session due to family or work crises, the printed manual cannot carry them; knowledge lives solely inside live facilitator download.

### The Month 13 Cliff
Module 10 spans just five pages (pp. 109–113): Impact Roadmap coaching, finalizing a template still prefilled with the identical 5+ Year aspiration (p. 111), `Sharing the Learning` (p. 112), and `Commissioning: You Are Culture Builders` (p. 113).

At Month 10, a church has barely completed *one* 3-month Beta (launched post-M6) while the 3-Year Milestones (`1 functioning 3rd-generation group`, `20%` participation) sit years away. Then external cohort pressure, monthly deadlines, and denominational coaching (`lmuniak@cdcma.org`) disappear overnight. In Month 13:
1. **Pastoral Turnover Breaks Continuity:** Because custom tools and processes grew organically inside a small lab team—and Leader Onboarding addresses *Beta leaders*, not incoming staff or new elders—if the Lead Pastor or Discipleship Champion transitions in Month 14, institutional memory vanishes.
2. **Budget & Calendar Gravity Reasserts Itself:** Without pre-negotiated budget reallocation or program subtraction, the first tight annual budget or busy fall ministry kickoff starves leader Soul Care and pulls staff back toward urgent weekend maintenance.
3. **Post-Beta Fatigue Meets the D2 Valley:** Moving beyond eager Beta early adopters (`2%`) toward the `16%` tipping point triggers the exact `D2/L2 "I quit"` friction `The MAP` predicts—right when the cohort safety net is gone.

## 5. Church-Ready Reconstruction Recommendations

To turn *Disciple Making Lab* from an inspiring workshop into a durable local-church transformation system, restructure five load-bearing elements:

1. **Flip the Sequence—Governance and `"What Is"` First (Modules 0–3):**
   * Require formal **Elder Board pre-cohort sign-off** before Module 1 so governance is inside the tent from Day 1.
   * Move `What's Your Path? Inventory` (M5) and `PLAN Inventory: "What Is"` (M8) into **Module 2**, paired with a mandatory **"Sacred Cow & Program Subtraction Audit"** tied directly to the `Moonshot` `STOP` marker.
   * Move the `Communication Folio` (`One-Pager` and `Stakeholders & Timeline`, M8) to **Module 3**—requiring elder, non-lab staff, and key lay-influencer alignment *before* custom tool creation begins.
2. **Cut Custom Tool Volume in Half to Break the Module 5–6 Bottleneck:**
   * Stop forcing busy teams to invent **5–6 custom tools** and build a shareable digital platform in 30 days. Master the **3 canonical tools** (`Bible Reading`, `Prayer`, `Jesus Pattern`) plus **at most 1 custom church tool** in Year 1. Provide pre-built digital templates so small churches don't stall on graphic formatting.
   * Split Module 6's overloaded immersion: dedicate **Module 6** exclusively to `Vehicle` design and `Beta` launch, and move `Leader Development` and `Culture-Making Grids` into **Module 7**.
3. **Co-Design Cross-Ministry Integration *Before* Betas Launch:**
   * Move `Tool Integration Practice` (M7) to **Module 5** and mandate that Small Groups, Worship, Children's, and Youth leaders co-author integration rather than retrofitting tools downstream.
   * Add an explicit **Legacy Small Groups Pathway Worksheet** (convert, hybridize, or sunset) in Module 6—and ban divisive visual status markers like Sunday leader lanyards.
4. **Embed Theological Guardrails & Leading Metrics:**
   * Connect Module 1's `Character & Competency Matrix` directly to Module 6 `Onboarding` via a written **Leader Vetting, Orthodoxy & Conflict-Escalation Protocol** before 2nd-generation multiplication begins.
   * Fix the blank `1-Year Objective` gap in Module 9 and track leading health indicators alongside `20%` targets: 90-day Beta retention, D2-to-D3 progression rates, Soul Care coach-to-leader ratios (capped at 1:4–5), `OUT` mission stories, and explicitly retired legacy programs.
5. **Replace the Module 10 "Commissioning" Cliff with a Year-2 Sustaining Architecture:**
   * Conclude Module 10 not with generic commissioning, but with **formal Elder Board ratification** of the Impact Roadmap, an **annual budget-line alignment**, and a codified **New Staff & Elder Onboarding Manual** capturing the church's definition, `PATH`, and tools so turnover never resets the clock.
   * Replace the abrupt Month 10 exit with **quarterly cohort coaching check-ins across Months 13–24** focused on navigating the jump from first-run Betas to 2nd- and 3rd-generation multiplication.
