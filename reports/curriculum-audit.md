**Prepared for:** Weston Muniak  
**Subject:** Current interactive presentation of the Disciple Making Lab (DML)  
**Primary sources audited:** `/workspace/index.html` and what it loads—especially `data/curriculum.js`, `data/tools.js`, `data/glossary.js`, and `js/app.js` (home, modules, facilitator guide, Year-2 Sustainability Hub)

---

## Executive Verdict

The current DML experience is a **theologically intentional, practice-first church-team lab**. It holds together a Christ-centered definition of a disciple, early personal obedience, elder-aligned pruning before scale, four napkin tools ordinary people can pass on, and a Year-2 continuity plan.

On its own terms the theology is sound. Justification and union with Christ sit under the habits. The Spirit is the agent of change, not a technique. Scripture is read in context toward Christ and then obeyed. The church is Word, Table, and elder oversight, not a peer franchise. Mission is cross-bearing love of named neighbors, practiced weekly once groups launch, and commissioned with Matthew 28. Where the lab already speaks Alliance language—Savior, Sanctifier, Healer, Coming King—it uses those names for this same work. An Alliance church can recognize that fit.

Pedagogically and pastorally, the design is credible for a motivated team of 4–8, including bivocational pastors, **if Module 4 actually frees the hours**. It will stall wherever leaders treat Modules 1–10 as content to finish, skip the pruning gate, or launch Module 8 Betas without hosts and elder alignment.

**Overall disciplemaking verdict:** Clear pathway, high intentionality, strong relational and obedience posture, credible reproduction design (2nd-generation co-leading by Beta weeks 6–8). Multiplication is designed, provided teams keep the weekly Beta cadence and the Soul Care caps.

## 1. Theological Audit

### How this pass judges

The lab is scored as Christian disciplemaking for a local church team: Is Christ the center? Is the gospel the foundation of the habits? Is Scripture authoritative in practice? Is the Spirit the one who gives growth? Is the church recognizable as Christ’s body under pastoral oversight? Is mission actual sentness, or a slogan?

Alliance fit is noted where the screen already uses Cross, Laver, Pitcher, and Crown, or where a local Alliance elder board would recognize the governance the lab asks for. Completeness against Simpson’s deeper-life crisis, healing as a denominational distinctive, global Alliance sending, or district polity is outside the test. Those absences are not findings.

### 1.1 Christ, the Gospel, and the Spirit

#### Christ the Savior, and grace under the habits — **Strong**
- Module 1’s destination begins with Christ’s call into relationship, with “justification and union with Christ” named as the foundation (chunk “Defining a Disciple”; glossary `justification`, `union-with-christ`).
- The Module 1 scenario rejects a chore-list definition (“reads the Bible, serves on Sunday, and invites a friend”) because it omits relationship with Jesus and the Spirit’s work.
- The key takeaway says consumer Christianity cannot be fixed with “a harder behavioral checklist.”
- Module 5 ties the 3-minute story and neighbor reconciliation to “Christ’s reconciling cross” (2 Cor 5:14–21) and tells storytellers to keep Jesus, not themselves, the hero.
- Module 10’s commissioning liturgy remembers “Jesus Savior (Cross)” at Word & Table, after Matthew 28:18–20 and 2 Timothy 2:1–2.

#### The Spirit and sanctification — **Strong as lived dependence**
- The disciple definition credits transformation to “the Lord, who is the Spirit” (2 Cor 3:18).
- Glossary `sanctification` refuses both fatalism and self-powered self-improvement, and locates change in beholding Christ in Scripture, prayer, repentance, suffering, and community (also Phil 2:12–13; 1 Thess 4:3; 5:23–24).
- Module 2’s Bible tool opens by asking the Spirit who inspired Scripture to open eyes to Jesus, and its guardrail forbids jumping to “what this verse feels like to me.”
- The Prayer Ring’s second beat is Confess & Lament, with pardon and forgiveness of others (Mt 6:12; 1 Jn 1:9).
- Growth Loop Stage 4 is **“Spirit-Dependent Maturity”**: abiding in the vine (Jn 15:5), with the explicit line that mature believers never graduate from confession, Soul Care, or dependence.
- Module 4 and Module 10 keep repeating that only God gives the growth (1 Cor 3:6–9; Jas 4:15). The “unstoppable discipleship machine” scenario is written to lose.

This is a real doctrine of sanctification for a practice lab: union, Spirit, repentance, abiding, and obedience. It does not need a separate holiness module to be orthodox or useful.

**Alliance fit:** The home card and Module 1 map Laver / Sanctifier onto “Spirit-led Scripture & union with Christ” and “indwells and purifies us by the Spirit.” An Alliance church will recognize that as Christ our Sanctifier at work in ordinary means. The lab does not teach a consecration crisis or “deeper life” as a distinct experience, and that is within its scope.

#### Christ restoring the broken — **Present as prayer, presence, and shepherding**
- The definition tool’s second movement is “Transformed & Restored by the Spirit (Laver / Sanctifier & Pitcher / Healer),” with the Spirit cleansing and restoring brokenness into Jesus’s likeness.
- The Prayer Ring’s Ask beat explicitly includes “daily needs, healing, protection from temptation.” The worked example prays for a neighbor facing surgery.
- The Jesus Pattern’s OUT movement is “teach, heal, and announce God’s kingdom” (Lk 6:17b–19). Module 3’s imitation example includes visiting a sick shut-in.
- Module 7 is badged “Pitcher (Healer) & Laver (Sanctifier).” Its Scripture set includes Isaiah 42:3 and James 5:13–16. The feel-outcome is “ready to sit in grief and failure alongside Christ our Healer.” The workshop role-plays sin relapse and bereavement, and a See More forbids confusing sin, suffering, and skill lag.
- Module 9 caps Soul Care at 1:4–5 and separates it from metric policing. Glossary `lament-endurance` cites Psalm 13, Romans 8, and James 5:13.

**Alliance fit:** The home card already defines the Pitcher as Friction Valley and Soul Care, and the Fourfold glossary glosses the Pitcher as “Christ our Healer who restores our brokenness,” citing James 5:14–16. That is the healing work this lab actually teaches: prayer for healing, presence with the sick and grieving, and patient care when someone relapses. An Alliance congregation can recognize it. A developed rite of anointing is not in the modules; the lab does not claim to be that lesson.

#### The Coming King, hope, and sentness — **Present as humble mission under Christ’s reign**
- The definition tool’s third movement is “On Mission With Christ (Crown / Coming King)”: denying self, loving neighbors, ambassadors of reconciliation (2 Cor 5:14–20; Lk 9:23). The napkin script ends, “we carry his cross daily ON MISSION with him as we love neighbors and await our Coming King.”
- Module 4 is badged “Crown (Coming King) Sober Stewardship,” with John 15, Mark 4:26–29, 1 Corinthians 3, and Paul’s hardship catalogue. The Stewardship Crossroads include “Step In” when God opens “a missionary door” (Col 4:3; also 1 Cor 16:9 in the tool).
- Module 10 reads James 4:13–15, 1 Corinthians 3:6–9, and Revelation 21:5, and refuses plans that mistake organizational momentum for grace.
- Commissioning names “Coming King (Crown)” at the Table.

**Alliance fit:** Crown, on this screen, is the King who governs hope and keeps mission humble. Local sentness under that King is thick (Section 1.2). The lab does not build a cross-cultural sending pipeline, and it does not need one to speak truthfully about Christ the Coming King.

### 1.2 Church and Mission

#### The church — **Word, Table, and real elder oversight**
- The Bowtie’s center is a “Sent Body (Word & Table),” nourished by Scripture, Baptism, and Communion. Module 1’s See More says Sunday worship should feed apprenticeship rather than compete with it. Module 9 then co-designs that center: Lord’s Prayer in the liturgy, silent Examen before Communion, Deuteronomy 6 fridge cards, baptism and Oikos stories instead of stage promos. The scenario rejects a “Disciple Lab Tool Time Promo.”
- Pre-Launch Gate: Elder Board covenant before Module 1. Module 1’s scenario refuses polishing plans for months before talking to elders.
- Module 6: elders, staff, and legacy hosts get a plain-language 1-Pager and a personal conversation before any public pilot.
- Module 9: Character & Competency (1 Tim 3; Ps 78:72); a vetting shield of Gospel Clarity, Integrity & Repentance, Relational Peace, and Elder Endorsement; escalation for abuse, mental-health crisis, marital breakdown, or persistent false teaching. On-screen line: “Peer leaders are companions, not licensed counselors or final church courts.”
- Module 10 and the Sustainability Hub: elder ratification within 30 days, a 2-page onboarding manual for new staff and elders, quarterly huddles, annual pruning.

Peer multiplication is strong, and the office is still visible. The drift to “anyone can launch groups” is what the vetting scenario is written to stop (the articulate newcomer who is hostile to elders is paused). The remaining risk is a team that clicks past Module 9, which is a pastoral failure, not a missing doctrine of office.

**Alliance fit:** An Alliance church will recognize an elder board that must covenant, endorse leaders, receive crisis calls, and ratify the roadmap. District superintendents, licensing, and credentialing are not discussed. The stated audience is a local team of 4–8, so that silence matches the scope.

#### Mission — **Local, theological, and built into the week**
- Module 5 Neighbor Table: pray by name for 3–5 people “who don’t yet know Christ” across live / work / learn / play; eat and listen; share a Before → Cross → Now story; offer one Gospel story. Person of Peace is Luke 10. Neighbors are “never targets.”
- Seeker mode uses four questions on a short Gospel text and, in the Luke 15 scenario, answers “Is God angry until we earn his love?” by sending the friend back to the father running down the road, plus a brief word about rescuing grace.
- Module 8 puts “Weekly OUT Mission” in every gathering, and the scenario rejects a syllabus that saves mission for week 4 of 4. The gathering-rhythm note says every week weaves “neighbor love and prayer for the lost” into ordinary life.
- Module 10 sends the team with Matthew 28 and the line “YOU ARE DISCIPLEMAKERS — GO MULTIPLY IN CHRIST.”
- The Culture Canvas mentions embedding the same simple tools in “hospitality, food pantry, missions, and deacon teams,” so existing missions teams are a place to practice the tools, not a blank the lab forgot.

This is a coherent missiology for a disciplemaking lab: reconciliation, named neighbors, discovery in the text, weekly obedience, and a commissioning under the risen Christ’s authority. Global deployment structures are a different curriculum.

**Set aside on this pass** (they do not survive as findings once C&MA completeness is no longer the test):

- “Thin Healer,” meaning the lab never teaches physical healing, anointing, or healing-as-distinctive. Prayer for healing and care of the sick and broken are in the tools and in Module 7.
- “Thin Coming King / missing global Alliance missions.” Crown is hope, stewardship, and sentness. Local mission is one of the lab’s thickest strands.
- “Missing deeper-life consecration” and “not a dedicated holiness module.” Sanctification is taught as Spirit-wrought, grace-based, and unending, and it is practiced in confession, abiding, and obedience.
- “Alliance polity is absent,” as a defect. Local elder government is present and serious. District process is outside this audience.

### 1.4 How Scripture and doctrine actually show up

Every module lists a Scripture set. Tools cite passages. Workshops practice a text. Commissioning closes on Matthew 28 and 2 Timothy 2.

The usual pattern is that a text **anchors a transferable practice** (Look • Listen • Live, the Prayer Ring, the Neighbor Table, the Growth Loop). Longer doctrinal sentences often live in glossary entries and “See More” layers. That matches a lab whose promise is practice. The primary chunks are not empty of doctrine: Module 1 states justification and union in the main definition chunk, Module 2 states the anti-proof-text rule in the main Bible chunk, and Module 10 states James 4:15 in the main planning chunk.

Orthodox instincts that are easy to miss and worth keeping:

- The Philippians 4:13 scenario forces context before impression.
- Module 3 protects Christ’s unique cross while still calling disciples to humble imitation.
- John 15 pruning is the Father’s work for fruit, and the tool forbids retiring ministries “with disdain for people who loved them.” Churches can still misuse it as a budget tactic; the text itself does not invite that.

### 1.5 Theological recommendations

No insert is required to repair the gospel, the Spirit, healing, the Coming King, or mission. The content already carries those.

1. **Keep the existing Jesus Pattern guardrail in the room.** When a team sketches UP / IN / DOWN / OUT, read the line already on the tool: Jesus’s death is not a productivity tip, and disciples announce his finished rescue. The warning is written; it should be spoken.
2. **Keep the 16% hedge in the room.** Module 1 already says the figure is descriptive. Facilitators should say that sentence out loud when the Bowtie percents are on screen.
3. **Optional, and not a repair:** one believer Micro-Triad example on a short non-narrative text (a psalm of lament, or a paragraph such as Ephesians 2:1–10) so LOOK is visibly the same method outside a story. Seeker groups should stay on the Gospel starters the lab already lists.

## 2. Pedagogical Audit — General Teaching & Learning

### 2.1 Learning design — **the promise is kept**

The home page names five dissemination principles, and the modules follow them:

1. **Chunked working memory** — modules break into 3–4 micro-lessons with read-times of about 2–4 minutes.
2. **Three-tier disclosure** — surface sentence, dotted glossary terms, “See More” for exegesis and examples.
3. **Dual coding** — 17 visual tools with napkin scripts and clickable parts, encountered inside the module that needs them, with a category badge (Core Tool #1–#4, then later leadership tools).
4. **Practice before systematizing** — Micro-Triads launch in Module 2; church-wide Betas wait until Module 8.
5. **Active retrieval** — two scenarios per module with immediate feedback, workbook prompts, and a field checklist.

**Adult-learning fit** is real: the Module 1 hook is “Why does busy church activity rarely produce spiritual grandchildren?”; later hooks are elder politics, pruning, neighbor awkwardness, biweekly drift, and the post-cohort cliff. The Facilitator Guide tells the coach to flip the room: 15-minute pre-read, then 90 minutes of debrief, sketch, role-play, and a named commitment.

**Outcomes** are Know / Feel / Do on every module. That remains one of the design’s clearest strengths.

**Sequence** still coheres: Modules 1–3 identity and personal tools; 4–6 honesty, witness, and alignment; 7–8 coaching and the weekly vehicle; 9–10 guardrails and continuity. Personal competence, then congregational truth-telling, then protected multiplication.

### 2.2 Retention and assessment — **formative on purpose**

| Mechanism | Quality |
|---|---|
| Napkin sketch drills (Module 2: draw both icons in 30 seconds and teach a partner) | High retention design |
| Teach-back and partner teaching | Strong |
| Two scenarios per module with corrective feedback | Good formative judgment checks |
| Field checklists tied to named people and dates | Behavioral retention |
| Saved local notes and module progress | Supports a team that meets across weeks |
| Module 7 “test with two ordinary laypeople” | Strong usability test of the tools |
| Module 9 vetting gate | A real readiness screen for leaders (gospel clarity, integrity, peace, elder endorsement) |
| Scored competency exam | Absent, and consistent with a lab that distrusts performance |

The prior wording “summative assessment weak/absent” overstated the hole. What is missing is a graded rubric. What is present is repeated public practice, a lay usability test, and a character gate before anyone leads a second-generation group. Module 8’s seven-point launch list is logistical (who, when, where, how long, co-leader, Soul Care pod, feedback dates). It does not ask a facilitator to watch a host do LOOK before LISTEN. That is a narrow gap inside an otherwise serious assessment design.

### 2.3 Load and engagement — **risks that still hold, one that was overstated**

**Still true:**

- **Seventeen diagrams exist.** A team that tries to make every one of them a member-facing tool will feel diagram-fatigued by Modules 7–10. The curriculum already limits that: Module 2 says live four tools first; Module 7’s napkin rubric allows at most one optional contextual card; Module 6 bans pulpit jargon (“Bowtie PATH Vehicle Beta”). The category badges already separate “Core Tool” from later leadership tools, so a new UI split is unnecessary. The risk is ignoring the rule the lab already states.
- **Insider names.** Bowtie, PATH/PLAN, Beta, Friction Valley, D1–D4. Module 6 exists to translate them. The Facilitator Guide’s “No Jargon” principle is the right ongoing discipline.
- **Two tracks at once.** Track A (triad and Oikos) runs from Module 2 while Track B (audit, stakeholders, pilots) builds. That is the right pedagogy. It is heavy only if pruning never funds it (see §4).

**Overstated last time:** “Sessions collapse if the 15-minute pre-read is skipped.” The workshop agendas are themselves the lesson: read Mark 2 together, pray the ring, sketch the napkin, book the triad. A cold team loses the glossary layer and some speed. The Facilitator Guide’s first 15 minutes are already “Celebrate & Honest Debrief,” which can carry a two-minute oral frame. Cold arrival is a quality dip, not a design break.

### 2.4 Pedagogy recommendations

1. **P1 — Add three observed lines to the Module 8 launch checklist** that is already on screen: host can open Celebrate without performing; host does LOOK before LISTEN; host can invite a neighbor without pressure. This uses the gate the lab already has.
2. **P2 — One spoken recap line in the facilitator template** when nobody pre-read: “Today we practice the tool, we do not tour it.” The template’s shape already supports this.
3. **Withdrawn:** a mandatory visual split between “4 Core Tools” and “Leadership Architecture Tools.” Category badges and the Module 2 / Module 7 rules already do that work.
4. **Withdrawn as a priority:** spaced retrieval quizzes at Modules 4, 6, 8, and 10. Field checklists and the opening debrief already retrieve the previous week’s practice. A quiz would fit the lab’s distrust of classroom Christianity poorly.

## 3. Pedagogical Audit — Biblical Teaching

### 3.1 How Scripture is taught — **inductive practice plus a memorized pattern**

- **Inductive, and primary:** Look • Listen • Live (who wrote it, plain meaning, what it shows of God and Christ, then Spirit and community, then a 48-hour step). Seeker discovery’s four questions. Live labs on Mark 2:1–12 and Luke 19. The Philippians 4:13 correction.
- **Patterned, and labeled as pattern:** the Jesus Pattern from Luke 6:12–19; TIM from 1 Corinthians 4 and 2 Timothy 2:2; Bowtie percentages; Growth Loop stages.

Both belong in a lay lab. The text stays in charge when the facilitator returns to the passage after the icon, which the tool steps already require (“summarize what happens in context”).

### 3.2 Faithfulness — **the anti-proof-text rule is in the main path**

On screen, not only in a footnote:

- LOOK before LISTEN, including the questions “Who wrote this to whom? What is the plain meaning?”
- Community testing (2 Pet 1:20).
- Christocentric reading (Lk 24:27; also Jn 5:39 and 2 Tim 3:15–17 in the glossary).
- A scenario that refuses “whatever impression hits me first.”
- The Bible tool’s own citation of 2 Timothy 3:16–17.

**Residual risks that still hold:**

- Organizational metaphors (funnel percentages, the napkin rubric, the 16% line) can catechize technique if a facilitator enjoys the diagram more than the text. The curriculum argues against that; it cannot force the room.
- DOWN in Luke 6 is a real observation of verse 17, and it is easy to stretch into a required four-beat spirituality. The guardrail in §1.3 is the right control. The pattern does not need to be withdrawn.

**Softened:** “Little training in literary context beyond the three icons.” LOOK already asks for author, audience, and plain meaning before Christ and application. The lab does not teach genre handbooks or manuscript marking. For a 60-second transferable method, that is the right depth.

### 3.3 Application — **obedience is concrete; hard texts are partly rehearsed**

**Still excellent:**

- Model, then try, inside the session.
- Partner teach-back of the icons.
- Seeker mode that keeps eyes on Jesus in the text (the Luke 15 father).
- A named person and a 48-hour window (James 1:22 is in the Module 2 Scripture set and on the tool).
- LISTEN is aimed at “a specific word, promise, or command that confronts our unbelief or idols.”

**Narrower than the prior finding:** the lab does rehearse contested readings. Philippians 4:13 is a prosperity misread. Luke 15 is “does God stay angry until we earn love?” Module 7 rehearses relapse and grief and tells coaches not to confuse sin with suffering. What it does not rehearse is a passage the believers themselves experience as morally costly (a judgment text, a sexuality text, an exclusivity challenge aimed at the group rather than at a parable). The method can carry that moment. The lab never walks a facilitator through one. That is a real, limited gap. It is not evidence that application avoids repentance. Confession is a weekly beat, and Module 1’s whole diagnosis is repentance from consumer Christianity.

### 3.4 Biblical pedagogy recommendations

1. **P2 — One worked believer example on a non-narrative text**, as in §1.5. Keep the five Gospel starters for seekers.
2. **P2 — If a church wants it, one facilitator note for a costly text:** do LOOK first, let the group say what offends, test impressions in the triad, and take a 48-hour step that may be repentance rather than a neighbor errand. The method already has those moves; the note would only show them on a harder passage.
3. **Withdrawn:** “Require a context paraphrase in every Tool Focus” as if the tool lacked it. The steps already say summarize context before LISTEN.
4. **Withdrawn:** an optional manuscript-marking track. It would break the napkin promise the lab is built on.

## 4. Pastoral Audit — Church Effectiveness and Doability

### 4.1 Why it still reads as parish-real

The curriculum keeps naming how church initiatives die:

- The audience line includes **bivocational** sustainability, and the Facilitator Guide says end on time and meet at kitchen tables.
- **Module 0** refuses a lone-ranger launch.
- **Module 4** funds new practices by subtraction (aim: 2+ hours a week), with honor for the people who loved the old program.
- **Module 6** gives veteran groups Convert / Hybridize / Bless & Sunset, and forbids Sunday badges that create a two-tier church.
- **Module 7** slows the timeline for relapse and grief.
- **Module 9** installs vetting, 24-hour elder escalation, and Soul Care caps, and tells Soul Care not to grade spreadsheets.
- **Module 10 and the Sustainability Hub** exist because cohorts die when the pastor leaves or fall calendars refill.

### 4.2 Time and staffing — **corrected, and still serious**

The on-screen load is a team session **per module**, plus about two hours a week of relational practice, and those two hours are supposed to be purchased by pruning.

A more accurate stack than “everything becomes weekly at once”:

| Practice | When it actually starts | Cadence in the text |
|---|---|---|
| Team lab session | Modules 1–10 | 90 minutes per module |
| Personal tool practice | Module 2 | A few short reps a week (the 2-minute prayer ring; LOOK three days) |
| Micro-Triad | Module 2 | 45 minutes, **1–4 times a month** |
| Oikos | Module 5 | Daily prayer by name; one real meal or coffee |
| Stakeholder coffees | Module 6 | A burst of 1-on-1s before pilots |
| Weekly Beta | Module 8 | 60–75 minutes **every week** for 12–16 weeks |
| Soul Care pod | Module 9 | **Monthly**, and only for leaders, capped at 4–5 |

**Judgment that still holds:** this is doable for a focused pilot team that prunes. It is not doable as an add-on beside an untouched midweek machine. The curriculum says so in Module 4’s pruning gate and in the load line itself. Many churches will agree in the room and decline in the calendar.

**Judgment that was too stacked:** treating triad, Beta, Soul Care, stakeholder coffees, and the team session as one permanent weekly pile. The heavy weekly add is the Beta, and it is gated behind pruning and alignment. Soul Care is monthly on purpose (Exodus 18 is cited so one pastor is not the sole shepherd).

**Staffing that still holds:** Module 9 needs enough mature people for 1:4–5 pods. A church with one overwhelmed pastor and no elder bandwidth will stall when apprentices appear. The text knows this; it cannot supply the people.

### 4.3 Format fit

| Format | Fit |
|---|---|
| Leadership team lab (4–8) | Primary intended format. Excellent. |
| Micro-Triad | Core vehicle from Module 2. Excellent. |
| Weekly home Beta (3–5) | Primary congregational vehicle from Module 8. Excellent. |
| Large classroom / midweek lecture | Intentionally de-centered. TIM is written against the Truth → Multiplication skip. |
| One-to-one only | Possible inside a triad. The curriculum prefers two or three so the leader cannot become the permanent priest of one person. |

### 4.4 Friction that is still real

1. **Pruning politics.** Stopping a legacy program threatens identity and donor memory. Module 6’s honor pathway is the pastoral answer; it does not remove the pain.
2. **Weekly cadence resistance.** The Module 8 scenario shows hosts will ask for every other Tuesday. The curriculum is right to refuse, for the reasons it gives: confession, crisis, and a mid-Beta handoff all die on a 14-day gap.
3. **Consulting flavor.** “Beta,” “Bowtie,” and “Friction Valley” will sound foreign in some rooms. Module 6’s plain-English swaps are the remedy, and they have to be used outside the lab room.
4. **Paper elder covenants.** Module 0 can be signed without heart. Module 6’s coffees and Module 10’s ratification are the later tests.
5. **Measurement anxiety.** Leading indicators are health-shaped (retention, tool fidelity, Oikos and baptism stories, Soul Care coverage, real pruning). The Soul Care warning is explicit. A culture can still ignore it.
6. **Living-room crises.** Escalation is specified for abuse, severe mental health, marital breakdown, and false teaching. Hosts need to hear that they are companions with a phone number, or they will either freeze or play counselor.
7. **Next-Gen co-design.** Module 9 assumes Worship, Kids, Youth, and Mercy leads are in the room. A siloed staff can nod and keep their own curriculum.

### 4.5 When it succeeds or fails

**Succeeds when** elders treat pruning as stewardship, the lead pastor is in a Micro-Triad, first pilots stay off the stage (Module 8), Soul Care stays non-policing, and Year-2 huddle dates are on the calendar before the commissioning applause.

**Fails when** staff consume the content without field practice, the lab is stacked on an unpruned calendar, pilots start before Module 6 alignment, character vetting is skipped for momentum, or the next pastor never sees the 2-page manual.

### 4.6 Pastoral recommendations

1. **P1 — One facilitator sentence that ties gates already in the curriculum:** do not open Module 8 Betas without a real Module 4 STOP, Module 6 elder alignment, and named hosts. Module 6’s field list already says “confirm which STOP has alignment to proceed cleanly.” The sentence only makes that abort visible in the guide.
2. **P2 — A stretched team-session option for truly bivocational calendars** (modules every other week) while Micro-Triads stay on their 1–4x rhythm and, once launched, Betas stay weekly. The stated load is already honest if pruning happens. This is an adoption aid, not a correction of a dishonest syllabus.
3. **P2 — Rehearse the 24-hour call, not only the relapse scene.** Module 7 already role-plays relapse and grief. Module 9 adopts the escalation protocol. One extra pass on “who do we phone tonight?” would lock the protocol into muscle memory.
4. **Withdrawn:** a new “red flag abort checklist” as if gates were missing. They are Modules 0, 4, 6, and 8.
5. **Withdrawn:** sample pruning scripts as a content gap. Module 6’s scenario already models the breakfast conversation with a 15-year host (thank them, offer Hybridize or Bless & Sunset, refuse a sudden shutdown).
6. **Withdrawn:** district or peer-cohort packaging. Year-2 huddles are the local church’s own calendar (Months 3, 6, 9, 12), which Module 10 already tells the team to book. A district program is a different project.

## 6. Cross-Cutting Strengths (Keep These)

1. **A Christ-centered definition of a disciple** that treats justification and union as the foundation and rejects consumer chore lists.
2. **Practice before program.** Tools are lived in Module 2; church-wide pilots wait until Module 8.
3. **Look • Listen • Live** as a transferable habit that puts context and Christ before impression.

5. **Legacy-group pathways** that make change ethically possible.
6. **Character-first vetting** and a real distinction between peer companionship and elder oversight.
7. **The Year-2 hub:** ratification, quarterly huddles, onboarding, annual pruning.
8. **A Facilitator Guide** that forbids a lecture-dominated 90 minutes.
9. **Mission as a weekly heartbeat** once Betas exist, aimed at named neighbors, with Jesus as the hero of the story.

## 7. Cross-Cutting Gaps (Watch These)

1. **Completion without costly practice.** The greatest danger is a team that finishes the screens, signs the covenant, and never prunes, never eats with a neighbor, and never hands Tool Focus to an apprentice.
2. **Concurrent load if pruning is fake.** The syllabus is sized for a team that frees about two hours. An unpruned calendar will break it at the Beta, not in the theology.
3. **Diagrams can outtalk the text.** Luke 6, the D-stages, and the Bowtie percents are well hedged and still easy to absolutize.
4. **Module 8 checks logistics more than observed skill.** Scenarios, teach-back, the lay tool test, and the vetting gate carry assessment. The launch list does not yet say “I watched this host do LOOK before LISTEN.”
5. **Costly texts are not walked through.** Misreading and parable-level gospel offense are rehearsed. A passage the group itself finds morally expensive is not.
6. **Jargon and paper compliance** can hollow out an otherwise serious design. Module 6 and the elder coffees are the antidote, and they are optional in practice even though they are mandatory on the page.

**No longer listed:** thin Healer, thin Coming King, missing global Alliance missions, missing deeper-life consecration, missing holiness module, missing Alliance polity appendix.

The current `index.html` experience is serious churchly disciplemaking: theologically awake, pedagogically literate, pastorally cautious, and built to reproduce.
