# Frontline Practitioner Critique: *Disciple Making Lab*

## 1. Executive Verdict

Let's cut straight to the bone: **the *Disciple Making Lab* does not produce multiplying disciplemakers—it produces church-staff systems architects and 10-month binders.**

Despite borrowing authentic grassroots vocabulary (*Relational Apprenticeship*, *back-of-a-napkin tools*, *life-on-life imitation*), this 10-module, 113-page curriculum from the C&MA Central District operates like an institutional change-management consultancy. Teams spend **five full months** analyzing culture, wordsmithing definitions, and inventing five to six bespoke visual diagrams before they ever launch a Beta group in Module 6—and spend Months 7–10 building 20-cell ministerial grids, sermon slogans, communication folios, and 5-to-20-year NASA roadmaps.

The curriculum tells on itself in two places. First, in Module 9's canonical 3-Year Milestone example, success after three years is **"At least 1 functioning 3rd-generation discipleship group"** (p. 101)—a glacial pace dressed up in "Moonshot" branding. Second, Module 10's final commissioning page doesn't read *"Go make disciples of all nations."* It reads: **"YOU ARE CULTURE BUILDERS"** (p. 113). In the trenches, building church culture and making disciples who make disciples are not the same thing. This lab excels at helping pastoral teams reorganize Sunday-centric machinery, while leaving the actual frontline art of turning skeptics into reproducing spiritual parents largely untouched.

## 2. What Actually Works in the Trenches

Before tearing down the scaffolding, credit belongs where the curriculum gets frontline realities right:

* **Naming the Attractional Dead-End (Bowtie Center, pp. 4, 19):** Putting `Attend (100%) -> Connect (60%) -> Serve (30%)` on one side and `2%` peer-to-peer multiplication at the center forces honest leaders to admit that busy church programs rarely produce spiritual grandchildren.
* **Action-Oriented Definition & 10 Practices (pp. 8–10):** Defining a disciple as *"One called by, in love with, becoming like, and on mission with Christ"* and pairing *"BEING WITH JESUS"* (character) with *"DOING THE THINGS JESUS COULD DO"* (competency) anchors discipleship in obedience, not just beliefs.
* **The Training Triangle Diagnosis (p. 23):** Framing `Information -> Imitation -> Innovation` rightly exposes why Western churches stall in classrooms: we confuse biblical data dumps with life-on-life transfer.
* **Situational Coaching via *The MAP* (p. 13):** Walking leaders through D1–D4 (`Directive: I do, you watch` → `Shepherding: I do, you help` → `Coaching: You do, I help` → `Empowering: You do, I cheer`)—and specifically naming D2's *"Valley of the shadow ('I quit')"* and D3's *"Co-dependence"* temptation—gives ordinary mentors real language for knowing when to carry weight and when to release it.
* **Vehicle Builder #2's 3-Part Rhythm (p. 69):** `#1 Celebrate` (*What was your next step from last time and what happened?*), `#2 Discipleship Focus`, and `#3 Next Step` (*one concrete action this week + accountability*) is genuine obedience-based DNA. If practiced weekly in a coffee shop, it works.
* **Relational Access & Soul Care (pp. 73, 78):** Including the Prayer of Examen and forcing leaders to ask, *"How will leaders know they have relational access to the life of the leaders investing in them?"* (p. 78), guards against treating people like ministry cogs.

## 3. Where This Breaks Down in Real Life ("Tearing It Apart")

### Pacing & Delay of Action: Five Months of Whiteboard Paralysis
Modules 1–5 span roughly five months of monthly cohorts without a single participant launching a real discipleship group. Look at the homework trail:
* **Month 1 (p. 15):** Gather six definitions of a disciple and draft one.
* **Month 2 (p. 27):** Re-interview two or three people for wordsmithing feedback and email the draft to `lmuniak@cdcma.org`.
* **Month 3 (p. 37):** Teach *one* starter tool to *one* person once for feedback.
* **Months 4–5 (pp. 45–61):** Invent, tweak to *"90% DONE,"* and test individual custom tools on single test subjects.

Even at the close of Module 6's two-day retreat (p. 80), the Beta still hasn't started—next steps are sending invites, formatting visual tools onto a *"shareable platform,"* and scheduling leader training. In frontline movement work, **delayed obedience kills relational momentum.** Whatever Spirit-led urgency was sparked in Module 1's Moonshot exercise (p. 14) calcifies into committee fatigue by Month 4. Worse, a five-month design runway creates brutal selection bias: evangelists and blue-collar practitioners tune out, leaving staff planners who love binders, grids, and staff meetings.

### Beta Standards That Guarantee Slow Stagnation
Module 6's Beta Checklist specifies a group lasting **"at least 3 months"** and meeting **"at least twice a month"** (p. 71). Do the math: twice a month over three months is **six meetings total**. Six meetings spaced 14 days apart is barely enough time to remember what happened last time, let alone confess hidden sin, navigate crisis, or transfer reproducible habits. Compounding that error, launching Betas after Month 6 means the 10-month Lab finishes in Module 10 right as the first six-meeting Beta barely wraps up—so **not one team validates 2nd- or 3rd-generation multiplication before graduating.**

### Institutional Bureaucracy Swallowing the Movement
From Module 7 onward, relational apprenticeship disappears behind church-wide marketing and project management: 20-cell `Culture-Making Grids` (`PLAN` × `Service, Groups, Kids, Youth, Other`, pp. 75–76, 84, 90, 108), `"Quickfire Language"` sermon catchphrases (pp. 85, 88), `Communication Folios` (`One-Pager`, `Stakeholders & Timeline`, pp. 92–93), and 5–20 year Apollo moonshot timelines (pp. 97–101). Look at the canonical 90-Day Goals on p. 101:
* *"Schedule DML Leadership monthly meetings for the year"*
* *"Start 3-6 disciple-making vehicle groups (Leaders will wear lanyards on Sundays; keep mentioning lanyards)"*
* *"Schedule quarterly Tool Time during services"*

Sunday lanyards and quarterly stage segments are not grassroots multiplication; they are church-staff change-management theater.

### Missing Grassroots Realities
* **Zero Pre-Believer Evangelism or Seeker DBS:** Across 113 pages, outreach appears as a single Vehicle Builder question (*"evangelism in the places they live, work, learn, and play,"* p. 68) and by slotting *"Week 4: Mission focus"* into a 4-week rotation (p. 70)—literally segregating mission into 25% of group life. There is no training on entering a skeptic's home, identifying a Person of Peace, telling a 3-minute story, or leading a Discovery Bible Study with people who reject Scripture's authority. Every tool assumes already-churched insiders.
* **No Map for Sin, Suffering, or Broken People:** Despite Spiritual SWOT (p. 5) and Soul Care (p. 78), the tone runs on corporate product sprints (*"NAME THE BABY!"* p. 68; *"90% DONE,"* p. 47) and NASA space launches (p. 99). There is no practical preparation for a disciple who relapses four times in D2, faces family rejection for following Jesus, or blows up a 90-day timeline through divorce or grief.
* **No Modeling Before Designing & Meetings Replacing Life:** Teams engineer a church-wide process before proving they can personally reproduce an apprentice. And apprenticeship is reduced to *"Design a 'Meeting'"* (p. 69) with 10-minute blocks, rather than shared meals, work errands, hospital waiting rooms, and real-time debriefs in everyday life.

## 4. Critique of Sequence, Tools & Custom Toolkit Mandate

### The Fatal "Custom 5–6 Tool Toolkit" Mandate (Modules 4–5)
Requiring every local church team to invent **5 or 6 proprietary visual tools** (p. 61) after minimal exposure to 3 canonical tools is **fatal over-engineering disguised as contextualization**.

First, **it violates the Lab's own Training Triangle (p. 23).** Teams barely touch *Information* in Modules 1–3, execute a single one-off feedback chat in Module 3 (p. 37), skip prolonged *Imitation* completely, and leap straight into *Innovation* in Modules 4–5 (`Tool #2` through `Tool #5`). Real back-of-a-napkin tools (*toolPhilosophy*, p. 41) are forged over years of scraping your knees with real seekers—never brainstormed in a 6-step workshop (p. 45) and uploaded to a `"shareable platform"` (p. 80).

Second, **bespoke 6-tool decks kill multiplication.** In the trenches, what transfers across four generations is three rugged habits an ordinary forklift driver can sketch on a Denny's napkin from memory—not a branded 6-diagram suite that requires Onboarding Training and Sunday `"Tool Time"` announcements to explain.

### Field-Test of the Core Models & Tools
* **Discipling Culture Master Tool (Bowtie, pp. 4, 19, 74):** Helpful diagnostic contrast between spectatorship and reproduction, yet structurally flawed because it leaves the attractional Sunday funnel (`100%/60%/30%`) as the permanent left lung of the movement and leans on `16%` org-systems tipping theory. In practice, `PLAN` swallows `PATH`.
* **The MAP (D1–D4 / L1–L4, p. 13):** Wise coaching lens, brittle if treated linearly. Real humans are frequently D4 in biblical literacy while remaining D1 in conflict, finances, or sharing faith with neighbors.
* **Training Triangle (p. 23):** Theologically and practically sound—undermined by a syllabus that lectures on imitation while skipping it.
* **Bible Reading Tool Circle (pp. 11, 31):** *The curriculum's sharpest grassroots tool.* Three bands (`Chew` / `Digest` / `Act`) intersected by the Holy Spirit arrow rightly force obedience (*"Do something"*). Weak spot: overly individualistic (*"stirring in me"*), missing a seeker entry point and an immediate *"Who will you pass this to?"* prompt.
* **Prayer Tool Wheel (p. 33):** Faithful to the Lord's Prayer (`Father` + 6 sectors + 3 pillars), though six slices plus three pillars can feel mechanical beside a weeping friend or an unchurched seeker learning to talk to God for the first time.
* **Jesus Pattern Tool (UP/IN/OUT, pp. 12, 35):** Napkin-ready and devastatingly clear—if used weekly to expose zero-OUT calendars rather than remaining a Module 1 & 3 lecture graphic.
* **Vehicle Builder Rhythm & Beta Criteria (pp. 68–72):** The 3-part obedience loop (`Celebrate / Focus / Next Step`) works; confining it to a bi-weekly `"Meeting"` with `"Week 4: Mission focus"` neutralizes its urgency.

## 5. Street-Level Reconstruction Recommendations

If we keep the Lab's best bones and rebuild for actual reproduction on the street, make five decisive cuts:

1. **Invert the Sequence—Obey First, Systematize Month 6:**
   Mandate that every cohort member gather 2–3 believers weekly *or* open a weekly Discovery Bible Study with unbelieving friends within **14 days of Module 1**. Spend Months 1–5 living inside real relational friction and coaching each other's failures; build institutional `PATH` and `PLAN` structures only out of field-tested scar tissue in Months 6–10.
2. **Abolish the 5–6 Custom Tool Mandate:**
   Cut Modules 4–5's tool-invention workshops entirely. Standardize around **three** proven habits—the **Bible Reading Circle** (adding *"Who will you tell?"*), **Jesus Pattern (UP/IN/OUT)**, and a stripped-down **Lord's Prayer**—plus **one** explicit pre-believer Oikos/Discovery tool. Enforce six months of *Imitation* before anyone invents a single diagram.
3. **Shift from Bi-Weekly "Meetings" to Weekly Life-on-Life + Daily Mission:**
   Eliminate the `"at least twice a month"` Beta floor (p. 71) and abolish `"Week 4: Mission focus"` (p. 70). Require **weekly** touchpoints using the 3-part obedience loop (`Celebrate / Focus / Next Step`, p. 69) embedded inside shared meals, errands, and hospitality—with weekly accountability for praying with and serving people far from God.
4. **Equip Leaders for D2 Messiness & Suffering:**
   Replace half of Module 7–8's matrix work with live case-study coaching on wounded, slow, and relapsing disciples—how to stay with someone in D2's *"Valley of the shadow"* when obedience stalls, without lowering Jesus's call or rushing a 90-day KPI.
5. **Gut the Lanyard-and-Folio Bureaucracy:**
   Trim 80% of the Culture-Making Grids, Communication Folios, and 20-year NASA charts. Replace `"At least 1 functioning 3rd-generation discipleship group"` in *Year 3* (p. 101) with expecting **every cohort team to walk with 2nd-generation reproducing disciples before Month 10 closes**—and rewrite Module 10's banner from **"YOU ARE CULTURE BUILDERS"** to **"YOU ARE DISCIPLEMAKERS—GO MULTIPLY."**
